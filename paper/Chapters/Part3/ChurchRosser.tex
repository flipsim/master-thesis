%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\chapter{The Church-Rosser Theorem}\label{ch:church-rosser}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% command for labels in commuting diagrams
\newcommand{\zb}[2][1em]{\makebox[#1]{$\displaystyle#2$}}

This chapter establishes the Church-Rosser theorem for $\piFD$. The theorem
states that if a process $P$ reduces to two distinct processes, $Q$ and $N$,
there always exists a common reduct $R$ such that both $Q$ and $N$ reduce to $R$.
Pictorially:

\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[
      row sep={1.5cm,between origins},
      column sep={1.5cm,between origins}
    ]
        & \zb{P} \arrow[dl, "\red"'] \arrow[dr, "\red"] & \\
    \zb{Q} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\red"'] &   & \zb{N} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\red"] \\
        & \zb{R} &
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

We adopt a proof strategy akin to Barendregt \cite{Barendregt1984:LambdaCalcAndTypes}
for the $\lambda$-calculus: we establish confluence for an intermediate relation
whose transitive closure coincides with reduction ($\red$).

Specifically, we prove confluence for the relation of \emph{parallel reduction}
($\parred$). Because confluence is preserved under transitive closure,
proving that $\parred^*$ and $\red$ coincide will immediately yield the
main result.

However, as noted in previous sections, structural congruence ($\equiv$) does
not preserve the exact set of redexes contained within a process. This complicates
the search for an appropriate $\parred$ relation, as we must ultimately prove
the following diamond:

\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[
      row sep={1.5cm,between origins},
      column sep={1.5cm,between origins}
    ]
        & \zb{P} \arrow[dl, "\equiv"'] \arrow[dr, "\parred"] & \\
    \zb{Q} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\parred"'] &   & \zb{N} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\equiv"] \\
        & \zb{R} &
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

In other words, any reduction available to a process must also be executable
in every other member of its structural equivalence class.
While standard reduction ($\red$) implicitly achieves this compatibility
via the \textsc{r-struct} rule, attempting to prove the Church-Rosser
property directly on $\red$ is overwhelmingly complex. The arbitrary
rearrangements permitted by $\equiv$ make it intractable to track exactly how
diverging redexes interact across equivalence classes.

To regain control over structural congruence and make the proof manageable,
we first introduce the auxiliary concept of \emph{simple reduction} ($\simplred$). To successfully
close the diamond above, we will subsequently rely on a technical device
which we refer to as \emph{directed congruence} ($\dircong$).

\begin{remark}
  We only prove confluence for well-typed processes because many intermediate
  results require channels to be used linearly.
\end{remark}

\section{Simple Reduction}
The crux of simple reduction is that it no longer relies on the rule \textsc{r-struct}
to expose redexes hidden within other members of the same equivalence class.
Instead, the notion of structural congruence is embedded directly into the
reduction rules.

To account for the commutation of links and cuts, $\simplred$ introduces
a symmetric counterpart for every rule of $\contr$. The rule \textsc{c-assoc},
however, requires special care due to its interaction with \textsc{r-axcut}.
Consider the following equivalence:
\[
  \parcomp{x}{y}{\parcomp{z}{w}{\link{z}{M}}{Q}}{P} \equiv
  \parcomp{w}{z}{Q}{\parcomp{x}{y}{\link{z}{M}}{P}}
\]
Here, \textsc{c-assoc} commutes scope restrictions in such a way that
\textsc{r-axcut} no longer applies across all structurally congruent variants.
Naively, one might extend $\simplred$ with computation rules of the following form:
\begin{align*}
  \parcomp{w}{z}{Q}{\parcomp{x}{y}{\link{z}{M}}{P}}
  &\simplred \parcomp{x}{y}{Q[M/w]}{P}
\end{align*}
However, if $P$ itself is a cut, structural congruence allows further
applications of \textsc{c-assoc}. This can push the link $\link{z}{M}$ even
further from its binder $(\nu w z)$, creating a situation that is not captured
by the naive rule above. To handle situations where a link is displaced
arbitrarily deep by repeated application of \textsc{c-assoc}, we introduce
\emph{evaluation contexts}.

\begin{definition}[Evaluation context] \label{def:evaluation-context}
  Evaluation contexts $\mathcal{E}$ are inductively defined by the following grammar:
  \[
    \mathcal{E} \Coloneqq \link{x}{\square} \mid \link{\square}{x} \mid \parcomp{x}{y}{P}{\mathcal{E}} \mid \parcomp{y}{x}{\mathcal{E}}{P}
  \]
\end{definition}

The grammar above does not ensure that the resulting context was created
through a series of \textsc{c-assoc} applications. This is why we additionally
define a well-formedness predicate for evaluation contexts.

\begin{definition}[Well-formedness of evaluation contexts] \label{def:wf-eval-ctx}
  An evaluation context $\mathcal{E}$ is well-formed at channel
  $x$, denoted $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x$, if the judgment can be derived
  by the following rules:
  \begin{center}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{}
      \infer1[\textsc{wf-link-l}]{\mathsf{WF} \, (\link{x}{\square})_x}
    \end{prooftree}
    \qquad
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{}
      \infer1[\textsc{wf-link-r}]{\mathsf{WF} \, (\link{\square}{x})_x}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x}
      \hypo{x \notin \fv(P)}
      \hypo{a, b \neq x}
      \infer3[\textsc{wf-cons-l}]{\mathsf{WF} \, (\parcomp{a}{b}{P}{\mathcal{E}})_x}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x}
      \hypo{x \notin \fv(P)}
      \hypo{a, b \neq x}
      \infer3[\textsc{wf-cons-r}]{\mathsf{WF} \, (\parcomp{b}{a}{\mathcal{E}}{P})_x}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{center}
\end{definition}

Assuming $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x$, the process
$\parcomp{y}{x}{P}{\mathcal{E}[M]}$ contains
a link $\link{x}{M}$ that can forward its message $M$ via \textsc{r-axcut}, provided
we transition to an appropriate member of its equivalence class.
However, given $P$, $\mathcal{E}$, $M$, and the names $x$ and $y$, we can
directly compute a possible reduct of $\parcomp{y}{x}{P}{\mathcal{E}[M]}$,
denoted $\mathsf{reduct}(E, M, P, x, y)$.

\begin{definition}[\textsc{axcut} reduct] \label{def:axcut-reduct}
  We define the reduct of an \textsc{axcut} determined by the process $P$,
  evaluation context $\mathcal{E}$, message $M$, and a channel with endpoints $x$ and $y$
  inductively by the following rules\footnote{Note that our formalization cannot define $\mathsf{reduct}$ through
  a structural recursion. $\mathcal{E}$ must be renamed in the recursive
  call, requiring a definition using well-founded recursion. For this purpose,
  we use Rocq's \enquote{Equations} plugin.}:
  \begin{align*}
    \mathsf{reduct}(\link{x}{\square}, M, P, x, y) &\coloneq P[M/y] \\
    \mathsf{reduct}(\link{\square}{x}, M, P, x, y) &\coloneq P[M/y] \\
    \mathsf{reduct}(\parcomp{a}{b}{Q}{\mathcal{E}}, M, P, x, y)
      &\coloneq \parcomp{a}{b}{Q}{\mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, P, x, y)} \\
    \mathsf{reduct}(\parcomp{b}{a}{\mathcal{E}}{Q}, M, P, x, y)
      &\coloneq \parcomp{b}{a}{\mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, P, x, y)}{Q}
  \end{align*}
\end{definition}

Using the notion of \textsc{axcut} reducts, we now define the relation of
simple reduction.

\begin{definition}[Simple reduction $\simplred$] \label{def:simplred}
  \begin{align}
    % reflexivity
    P &\simplred P
    \tag{\textsc{sr-refl}}
    \\
    % tensor/par l
    \link{\ssend{v.P}{z.Q}}{\sreceive{a}{b}{R}}
    &\simplred \parcomp{b}{z}{\parcomp{a}{v}{R}{P}}{Q}
    \tag{\textsc{sr-$\otimes\parr$-l}}
    \\
    % tensor/par r
    \link{\sreceive{a}{b}{R}}{\ssend{v.P}{z.Q}}
    &\simplred \parcomp{b}{z}{\parcomp{a}{v}{R}{P}}{Q}
    \tag{\textsc{sr-$\otimes\parr$-r}}
    \\
    % plus/with 1 l
    \link{\slselect{z.P}}{\schoice{v.Q}{w.R}}
    &\simplred \parcomp{z}{v}{P}{Q}
    \tag{\textsc{sr-$\oplus\with_1$-l}}
    \\
    % plus/with 1 r
    \link{\schoice{v.Q}{w.R}}{\slselect{z.P}}
    &\simplred \parcomp{z}{v}{P}{Q}
    \tag{\textsc{sr-$\oplus\with_1$-r}}
    \\
    % plus/with 2 l
    \link{\srselect{z.P}}{\schoice{v.Q}{w.R}}
    &\simplred \parcomp{z}{w}{P}{R}
    \tag{\textsc{sr-$\oplus\with_2$-l}}
    \\
    % plus/with 2 r
    \link{\schoice{v.Q}{w.R}}{\srselect{z.P}}
    &\simplred \parcomp{z}{w}{P}{R}
    \tag{\textsc{sr-$\oplus\with_2$-r}}
    \\
    % 1/bot l
    \link{\sclose}{\swait{P}}
    &\simplred P
    \tag{\textsc{sr-$1\bot$}}
    \\
    % 1/bot r
    \link{\swait{P}}{\sclose}
    &\simplred P
    \tag{\textsc{sr-$1\bot$-r}}
    \\
    % Seq
    P \seq xS
    &\simplred P[S/x]
    \tag{\textsc{sr-seq}}
  \end{align}
  \vspace{-2.0em}
  \begin{center}
    % axcut l
    \hfill
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x}
      \infer1[]{\parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}[M]}{P} \simplred \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, P, x, y)}
    \end{prooftree}
    \hfill (\textsc{sr-axcut-l})

    \bigskip

    % axcut r
    \hfill
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x}
      \infer1[]{\parcomp{y}{x}{P}{\mathcal{E}[M]} \simplred \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, P, x, y)}
    \end{prooftree}
    \hfill (\textsc{sr-axcut-r})

    \bigskip

    % congruence: cut l
    \hfill
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \simplred P'}
      \infer1[]{\parcomp{x}{y}{P}{Q} \simplred \parcomp{x}{y}{P'}{Q}}
    \end{prooftree}
    \hfill (\textsc{sr-cong-cut-l})

    \bigskip

    % congruence: cut r
    \hfill
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{Q \simplred Q'}
      \infer1[]{\parcomp{x}{y}{P}{Q} \simplred \parcomp{x}{y}{P}{Q'}}
    \end{prooftree}
    \hfill (\textsc{sr-cong-cut-r})

    \bigskip

    % congruence: seq
    \hfill
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \simplred P'}
      \infer1[]{P \seq s \simplred P' \seq s}
    \end{prooftree}
    \hfill
    (\textsc{sr-cong-seq})
  \end{center}
\end{definition}


\section{Parallel Reduction}
Having introduced $\simplred$, we now define parallel reduction.

\begin{definition}[\rocqicon~Parallel reduction $\parred$] \label{def:parred}
  Parallel reduction is defined as the union of simple reduction and structural congruence.
  \[
    \parred \; \coloneq \; \simplred \; \cup \; \equiv
  \]
\end{definition}

First, we prove that for any two process $P$ and $Q$, we have $P \red Q$ if and only if
$P \parred^* Q$. The direction $(\Rightarrow)$ is straightforward.

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~$\red$ is a subset of $\parred^*$] \label{lemma:red-subset-parred-star}
  Reduction is contained in the transitive closure of parallel reduction.
  \[
    \red \; \subset \; \parred^*
  \]
\end{lemma}

For the direction $(\Leftarrow)$, we observe that the result of computing
an \textsc{axcut} reduct can be obtained using $\equiv$ and $\contr$.

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~An \textsc{axcut} reduct lies in $\red$] \label{lemma:simplred-axcut-in-red}
  Let $Q$ be a process such that $Q = \mathcal{E}[M]$ and $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x$.
  There exists a process $L$ such that
  \[
    \parcomp{x}{y}{Q}{P} \equiv L \quad {and} \quad
    L \contr \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, P, x, y).
  \]
\end{lemma}

Using Lemma \ref{lemma:simplred-axcut-in-red}, we prove the following results.

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~$\simplred$ is a subset of $(\contr \; \cup \; \equiv)$] \label{lemma:simplred-in-contr-cup-equiv}
  Simple reduction is contained in the union of contraction and structural congruence.
  \[
    \simplred \; \subset \; \contr \; \cup \; \equiv
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[\rocqicon~$\parred$ is a subset of $\red$] \label{cor:parred-in-red}
  Parallel reduction is contained in the relation of reduction.
  \[
    \parred \; \subset \; \red
  \]
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~$\parred^*$ and $\red$ coincide] \label{lemma:parred-red-coincides}
  $P \parred^* Q$ if and only if $P \red Q$.
\end{lemma}

What remains is to prove that $\parred$ possesses the diamond property.
If $P \parred Q$ and $P \parred N$, then there are three cases to consider.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $P \simplred Q$ and $P \simplred N$.
  \item $P \simplred Q$ and $P \equiv N$, or $P \equiv Q$ and $P \simplred N$.
  \item $P \equiv Q$ and $P \equiv N$.
\end{enumerate}

We will prove the following three diamonds, corresponding to the three cases above.

\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.3\textwidth}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}[
        row sep={1.5cm,between origins},
        column sep={1.5cm,between origins}
      ]
          & \zb{P} \arrow[dl, "\simplred"'] \arrow[dr, "\simplred"] & \\
      \zb{Q} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\parred"'] &   & \zb{N} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\parred"] \\
          & \zb{R} &
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{minipage}
\begin{minipage}{0.3\textwidth}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}[
        row sep={1.5cm,between origins},
        column sep={1.5cm,between origins}
      ]
          & \zb{P} \arrow[dl, "\equiv"'] \arrow[dr, "\simplred"] & \\
      \zb{Q} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\simplred"'] &   & \zb{N} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\parred"] \\
          & \zb{R} &
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{minipage}
\begin{minipage}{0.3\textwidth}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}[
        row sep={1.5cm,between origins},
        column sep={1.5cm,between origins}
      ]
          & \zb{P} \arrow[dl, "\equiv"'] \arrow[dr, "\equiv"] & \\
      \zb{Q} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\equiv"'] &   & \zb{N} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\equiv"] \\
          & \zb{R} &
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

The diamond property for $\parred$ is an immediate consequence of these three
intermediate results. We observe that the third case follows trivially from
the symmetry of structural congruence. The remaining cases, however,
are more elaborate.

\subsection{Diverging Simple Reductions}

To prove the first diamond, we first state a few properties about simple reduction
and evaluation contexts.

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~Simple reduction is invariant under substitution] \label{lemma:simplred-invar-subst}
  Let $\Gamma \vdash P$ be a well-typed process. If $P \simplred P'$, then
  $P\sigma \simplred P'\sigma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~\textsc{axcut} reducts are invariant under reduced evaluation contexts] \label{lemma:reduct-invar-red-ctx}
  Let $\Gamma \vdash Q$ and $\Gamma' \vdash P$ be well-typed processes such that $Q = \mathcal{E}[M]$
  and $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x$. If $Q \simplred Q'$, then one of the following holds:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item There exists $\mathcal{E}'$ and $M'$ such that $Q' = \mathcal{E}'[M']$,
          $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}'_x$ and
          \[
            \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, P, x, y) \simplred
            \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}', M', P, x, y),
          \]
          or
    \item $\mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, P, x, y) \equiv \parcomp{x}{y}{Q'}{P}$
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~\textsc{axcut} reducts are invariant under reduced substituent] \label{lemma:reduct-invar-substituent}
  Let $\Gamma \vdash P$ be a well-typed process. If $P \simplred P'$, then
  \[
    \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, P, x, y) \simplred
    \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, P', x, y).
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~Uniqueness of evaluation contexts] \label{lemma:uniq-eval-ctx}
  Let $\mathcal{E}$ and $\mathcal{E}'$ be evaluation contexts such that
  $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x$ and $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}'_x$.
  If $\Gamma \vdash \mathcal{E}[M]$ and $\mathcal{E}[M] = \mathcal{E}'[M']$, then $\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{E}'$ and
  $M = M'$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~Confluence for \textsc{axcut} reduct pairs] \label{lemma:critical-pairs}
  Let $\mathcal{E}$ and $\mathcal{E}'$ be evaluation contexts such that
  $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x$ and $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}'_y$.
  If $x \notin \fv(M)$ and $y \notin \fv(M')$, then
  \[
    \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, \mathcal{E}'[M'], x, y) \equiv
    \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}', M', \mathcal{E}[M], y, x).
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}
  Lemma \ref{lemma:critical-pairs} highlights a property that distinguishes a $\pi$-calculus
  like $\piFD$ from classical sequent calculi.
  The equivalence described in the conclusion
  is the result of two possible reductions of a critical pair. In $\piFD$,
  these two reducts turn out to be structurally congruent. Intuitively, an evaluation context
  can be viewed as a list of processes. The expression
  \[
    \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, \mathcal{E}'[M'], x, y)
  \]
  thus represents the concatenation of two such lists.
  Because structural congruence allows us to ignore the concrete order of processes in
  a parallel composition, the order in which these lists are joined becomes immaterial.
\end{remark}

Using these auxiliary results, we can now show that diverging simple reductions
can always be reconciled.

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~Reconciling diverging simple reductions] \label{lemma:roncile-simplered}
  Let $\Gamma \vdash P$ be a well-typed process.
  If $P \simplred Q$ and $P \simplred N$, then $Q \parred R$ and $N \parred R$
  for some $R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By induction on the derivation of $P \simplred Q$.
  The cases \textsc{sr-refl} and all $\beta$-reductions are straightforward.
  For the remaining cases, we proceed as follows.

  \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1em, label=Case:, leftmargin=*]
    \item (\textsc{sr-seq}) \\
      The last rule of the derivation of $P \simplred N$ may be one of the following:
      \textsc{sr-refl}, \textsc{sr-seq} or \textsc{sr-cong-seq}. For the first two,
      the result follows trivially. For the latter, we are looking at the following situation:
      \[
         P = Q' \seq xS \simplred Q'[S/x] = Q \quad \text{and} \quad
         P = Q' \seq xS \simplred Q'' \seq xS = N \quad
      \]
      with $Q' \simplred Q''$. Thus, using Lemma \ref{lemma:simplred-invar-subst},
      we have $Q'[S/x] \simplred Q''[S/x]$. Therefore,
      \[
        Q = Q'[S/x] \parred Q''[S/x] = R
      \]
      and
      \[
        N = Q'' \seq xS \parred Q''[S/x] = R.
      \]

    \item (\textsc{sr-cong-seq}) \\
      Again, the last rule of the derivation of $P \simplred N$ may be one of the following:
      \textsc{sr-refl}, \textsc{sr-seq} or \textsc{sr-cong-seq}. The case \textsc{sr-refl} follows trivially.
      If the derivation ends with \textsc{sr-seq}, we argue using Lemma
      \ref{lemma:simplred-invar-subst} as above. Lastly, for \textsc{sr-cong-seq},
      we have
      \[
        P = Q' \seq xS \simplred Q_1 \seq xS = Q \quad \text{and} \quad
        P = Q' \seq xS \simplred Q_2 \seq xS = N \quad
      \]
      with $Q' \simplred Q_1$ and $Q' \simplred Q_2$. Thus, by induction hypothesis,
      there exists $Q''$ s.t. $Q_1 \parred Q''$ and $Q_2 \parred Q''$.
      The result follows.

    \item (\textsc{sr-cong-cut-l}) \\
      An inversion on the premise $P \simplred N$ yields the subcases
      \textsc{sr-refl}, \textsc{sr-axcut-l}, \textsc{sr-axcut-r}, \textsc{sr-cong-cut-l},
      and \textsc{sr-cong-cut-r}. We only highlight the cases
      \textsc{sr-axcut-l} and \textsc{sr-axcut-r}. The other cases follow
      immediately or by application of the induction hypothesis.
      \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1em, label=Subcase:]
        \item (\textsc{sr-axcut-l}) \\
          We have
          \[
            P = \parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}[M]}{Q'} \simplred \parcomp{x}{y}{P'}{Q'} = Q
          \]
          with $\mathcal{E}[M] \simplred P'$ and
          \[
            P = \parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}[M]}{Q'} \simplred \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, Q', x, y) = N.
          \]
          By Lemma \ref{lemma:reduct-invar-red-ctx}, we consider two cases.

          First, we may have $P' = \mathcal{E}'[M']$ for some $\mathcal{E}'$ and $M'$
          such that $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}'_x$ and
          \[
            \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, Q', x, y) \simplred \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}', M', Q', x, y).
          \]
          Thus,
          \[
            Q = \parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}'[M']}{Q'}
            \parred \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}', M', Q', x, y)
          \]
          and
          \[
            N = \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, Q', x, y) \parred \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}', M', Q', x, y).
          \]

          Second, we may have $\mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, Q', x, y) \equiv \parcomp{x}{y}{P'}{Q'}$.
          The result follows immediately.
        \item (\textsc{sr-axcut-r}) \\
          We have
          \[
            P = \parcomp{x}{y}{Q'}{\mathcal{E}[M]} \simplred \parcomp{x}{y}{Q''}{\mathcal{E}[M]} = Q
          \]
          and
          \[
            P = \parcomp{x}{y}{Q'}{\mathcal{E}[M]} \simplred \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, Q', x, y) = N.
          \]
          The result follows using Lemma \ref{lemma:reduct-invar-substituent}.
      \end{enumerate}
    \item (\textsc{sr-cong-cut-r}) \\
      Analogous to the previous case.

    \item (\textsc{sr-axcut-l}) \\
      An inversion on the premise $P \simplred N$ yields five subcases. We
      only highlight the cases \textsc{sr-axcut-l} and \textsc{sr-axcut-r}.
      The other cases are similar to previous ones.
      \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1em, label=Subcase:]
        \item (\textsc{sr-axcut-l}) \\
          By the uniqueness of evaluation contexts (Lemma \ref{lemma:uniq-eval-ctx}),
          we already have $Q = N$. The result follows trivially.

        \item (\textsc{sr-axcut-r}) \\
          We are looking at the following situation:
          \begin{align*}
            P &= \parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}[M]}{\mathcal{E'}[M']} \\ & \simplred
              \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, \mathcal{E'}[M'], x, y) = Q
          \end{align*}
          and
          \begin{align*}
            P & = \parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}[M]}{\mathcal{E'}[M']} \\ & \simplred
              \mathsf{reduct}(E', M', \mathcal{E}[M], y, x) = N
          \end{align*}
          with $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x$ and $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}'_y$.
          The results follow using Lemma \ref{lemma:critical-pairs}, since $M \equiv N$.
      \end{enumerate}
    \item (\textsc{sr-axcut-r}) \\
      Analogous to the previous case.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Compatibility of Simple Reduction with Structural Congruence}
To prove that simple reduction is compatible with structural congruence,
we introduce the relation of \emph{directed congruence} ($\dircong$).
Unlike standard structural congruence ($\equiv$), directed congruence is neither
transitive nor symmetric by definition.
Omitting symmetry ensures that the relation remains syntax-directed, avoiding
circular derivations during rule-inductive proofs.
Although symmetry is not built into the relation itself, we can show that it is admissible.

Together, these two properties allow us to establish the following commutation
between directed congruence and simple reduction:
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[
      row sep={1.5cm,between origins},
      column sep={1.5cm,between origins}
    ]
        & \zb{P} \arrow[dl, "\dircong"'] \arrow[dr, "\simplred"] & \\
    \zb{Q} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\simplred"'] &   & \zb{N} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\equiv"] \\
        & \zb{R} &
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

Because structural congruence corresponds to the reflexive-transitive closure of
directed congruence ($\dircong^*$), iterating this property yields the desired
compatibility result for parallel reduction:
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[
      row sep={1.5cm,between origins},
      column sep={1.5cm,between origins}
    ]
        & \zb{P} \arrow[dl, "\equiv"'] \arrow[dr, "\parred"] & \\
    \zb{Q} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\parred"'] &   & \zb{N} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\equiv"] \\
        & \zb{R} &
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
This serves as a generalization of the second diamond above.

\begin{definition}[Directed congruence $\dircong$] \label{def:dircong}
  The relation $\dircong$ is (mutually) inductively defined by the following rules.

  \begin{center}
    \begin{prooftree}[center=false]
      \hypo{}
      \infer1[\textsc{dc-nil}]{\Lambda \dircong \Lambda}
    \end{prooftree}
    \quad
    \begin{prooftree}[center=false]
      \hypo{M_{l1} \Rrightarrow M_{l2}}
      \hypo{M_{r1} \Rrightarrow M_{r2}}
      \infer2[\textsc{dc-link}]{\link{M_{l1}}{M_{r1}} \Rrightarrow \link{M_{r2}}{M_{l2}}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{M_{l1} \Rrightarrow M_{l2}}
      \hypo{M_{r1} \Rrightarrow M_{r2}}
      \infer2[\textsc{dc-cong-link}]{\link{M_{l1}}{M_{r1}} \Rrightarrow \link{M_{l2}}{M_{r2}}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{Q \dircong Q'}
      \infer2[\textsc{dc-cut-comm}]{\parcomp{x}{y}{P}{Q} \dircong \parcomp{y}{x}{Q'}{P'}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{Q \dircong Q'}
      \infer2[\textsc{dc-cong-cut}]{\parcomp{x}{y}{P}{Q} \dircong \parcomp{x}{y}{P'}{Q'}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \Rrightarrow P'}
      \hypo{Q \Rrightarrow Q'}
      \hypo{R \Rrightarrow R'}
      \infer3[\textsc{dc-assoc-l}]{\parcomp{v}{z}{\parcomp{x}{y}{P}{Q}}{R} \dircong \parcomp{x}{y}{P'}{\parcomp{v}{z}{Q'}{R'}}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \Rrightarrow P'}
      \hypo{Q \Rrightarrow Q'}
      \hypo{R \Rrightarrow R'}
      \infer3[\textsc{dc-assoc-r}]{\parcomp{x}{y}{P}{\parcomp{v}{z}{Q}{R}} \dircong \parcomp{v}{z}{\parcomp{x}{y}{P'}{Q'}}{R'}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{S \dircong S'}
      \infer2[\textsc{dc-cong-seq}]{P \seq xS \dircong P' \seq xS'}
    \end{prooftree}
    \quad
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{}
      \infer1[\textsc{dc-refl-msg}]{M \dircong M}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \infer1[\textsc{dc-$\oplus$-l}]{\slselect{P} \dircong \slselect{P'}}
    \end{prooftree}
    \quad
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \infer1[\textsc{dc-$\oplus$-r}]{\srselect{P} \dircong \srselect{P'}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{Q \dircong Q'}
      \infer2[\textsc{dc-$\with$}]{\schoice{x.P}{y.Q} \dircong \schoice{x.P'}{y.Q'}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{Q \dircong Q'}
      \infer2[\textsc{dc-$\otimes$}]{\ssend{x.P}{y.Q} \dircong \ssend{x.P'}{y.Q'}}
    \end{prooftree}
    \quad
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \infer1[\textsc{dc-$\parr$}]{\sreceive{x}{y}{P} \dircong \sreceive{x}{y}{P'}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}[center=false]
      \hypo{}
      \infer1[\textsc{dc-$1$}]{\sclose \dircong \sclose}
    \end{prooftree}
    \quad
    \begin{prooftree}[center=false]
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \infer1[\textsc{dc-$\bot$}]{\swait{P} \dircong \swait{P'}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{center}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~$\dircong$ is a subset of $\equiv$] \label{lemma:dircong-in-equiv}
  If $P \dircong Q$, then $P \equiv Q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~$\equiv$ is a subset of $\dircong^*$] \label{lemma:equiv-in-dircong-star}
  If $P \equiv Q$, then $P \dircong^* Q$.
\end{lemma}

We extend the notion of directed congruence to evaluation contexts and substitutions as well.

  \begin{center}

      \infer1[\textsc{edc-nil-refl-l}]{\link{x}{\square} \dircong \link{x}{\square}}

      \hypo{}
      \infer1[\textsc{edc-nil-refl-r}]{\link{\square}{x} \dircong \link{\square}{x}}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'}
      \infer2[\textsc{edc-cong-cons-r}]{\parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}}{P} \dircong \parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}'}{P'}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'}
      \infer2[\textsc{edc-comm-l}]{\parcomp{x}{y}{P}{\mathcal{E}} \dircong \parcomp{y}{x}{\mathcal{E}'}{P'}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{Q \dircong Q'}
      \hypo{\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'}
      \infer3[\textsc{edc-assoc-rl}]{\parcomp{v}{z}{\parcomp{x}{y}{P}{\mathcal{E}}}{Q} \dircong \parcomp{x}{y}{P'}{\parcomp{v}{z}{\mathcal{E}'}{Q'}}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{Q \dircong Q'}
      \hypo{\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'}
      \infer3[\textsc{edc-assoc-rr}]{\parcomp{v}{z}{\parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}}{P}}{Q} \dircong \parcomp{v}{z}{\mathcal{E}'}{\parcomp{x}{y}{P'}{Q'}}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{Q \dircong Q'}
      \hypo{\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'}
      \infer3[\textsc{edc-assoc-lr}]{\parcomp{x}{y}{P}{\parcomp{v}{z}{\mathcal{E}}{Q}} \dircong \parcomp{v}{z}{\parcomp{x}{y}{P'}{\mathcal{E}'}}{Q'}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{Q \dircong Q'}
      \hypo{\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'}
      \infer3[\textsc{edc-assoc-ll}]{\parcomp{x}{y}{P}{\parcomp{v}{z}{Q}{\mathcal{E}}} \dircong \parcomp{x}{y}{\parcomp{v}{z}{P'}{Q'}}{\mathcal{E}'}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{Q \dircong Q'}
      \hypo{\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'}
      \infer3[\textsc{edc-assoc-cut-l}]{\parcomp{v}{z}{\parcomp{x}{y}{P}{Q}}{\mathcal{E}} \dircong \parcomp{x}{y}{P'}{\parcomp{v}{z}{Q'}{\mathcal{E}'}}}
    \end{prooftree}

    \bigskip

    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{P \dircong P'}
      \hypo{Q \dircong Q'}
      \hypo{\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'}
      \infer3[\textsc{edc-assoc-cut-r}]{\parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}}{\parcomp{v}{z}{P}{Q}} \dircong \parcomp{v}{z}{\parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}'}{P'}}{Q'}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{center}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Directed congruence for substitutions] \label{def:dircong-for-subst}
  Given substitutions $\sigma_1 = [M_1, \hdots, M_n / x_1, \hdots, x_n]$
  and $\sigma_2 = [M_1', \hdots, M_n' / x_1, \hdots, x_n]$, we write
  $\sigma_1 \dircong \sigma_2$ if $M_i \dircong M_i'$ for every $i$.
\end{definition}

We first state a few properties about directed congruence and simple reduction.

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~Directed congruence is invariant under substitution] \label{lemma:dircong-invar-subst}
  \mbox{}
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item If $P \dircong P'$ and $\sigma_1 \dircong \sigma_2$, then $P\sigma_1 \dircong P'\sigma_2$.
    \item If $M \dircong M'$ and $\sigma_1 \dircong \sigma_2$, then $M\sigma_1 \dircong M'\sigma_2$.
    \item If $S \dircong S'$ and $\sigma_1 \dircong \sigma_2$, then $S\sigma_1 \dircong S'\sigma_2$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~\textsc{axcut} reducts are invariant under directed congruence] \label{lemma:reduct-inver-dircong}
  Let $\Gamma \vdash Q$ be a well-typed process such that $Q = \mathcal{E}[M]$
  and $\mathsf{WF} \, \mathcal{E}_x$.
  If $P \dircong P'$ and $Q \dircong R$, then there exist $\mathcal{E}'$ and $M'$ such that:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item $R = \mathcal{E}'[M']$,
    \item $\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'$, and
    \item $\mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, P, x, y) \dircong \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}', M', P', x, y)$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Using Lemmata \ref{lemma:dircong-invar-subst} and \ref{lemma:reduct-inver-dircong},
we can now prove the first result relating simple reduction and structural congruence.

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~Compatibility of simple reduction with directed congruence] \label{lemma:compat-simplred-dircong}
  Let $\Gamma \vdash P$ be a well-typed process.
  If $P \dircong Q_1$ and $P \simplred Q_2$, then $Q_1 \simplred R$ and $Q_2 \equiv R$
  for some $R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
  By induction on the derivation of $P \dircong Q_1$. The cases
  \textsc{dc-nil}, \textsc{dc-link}, and \textsc{dc-cong-link} follow straightforwardly
  by case analysis on the derivation $P \simplred Q_2$.

  We omit the cases \textsc{dc-assoc-l} and \textsc{dc-assoc-r}.
  Because associativity rearranges the process tree,
  analyzing these cases requires a cascade of nested inversions
  on the sub-derivations of $P \simplred Q_2$.
  The complete, formalized proof can be found in the accompanying Rocq development.
  Instead, we only highlight those cases that demonstrate how the previously established
  results come into play.

  \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1em, label=Case:, leftmargin=*]
    \item (\textsc{dc-cong-seq}) \\
      A case analysis on the last rule of $P \simplred Q_2$ yields three subcases,
      where the case \textsc{dc-refl} follows trivially.
      \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1em, label=Subcase:]
        \item (\textsc{sr-seq}) \\
          We have
          \[
            P = Q \seq xS \dircong Q' \seq xS' = Q_1 \simplred Q'[S'/x]
          \]
          with $Q \dircong Q'$ and $S \dircong S'$, and
          \[
            P = Q \seq xS \simplred Q[S/x] \dircong Q'[S'/x]
          \]
          where we use Lemma \ref{lemma:dircong-invar-subst} for the last step.
        \item (\textsc{sr-cong-seq}) \\
          We have $P = Q \seq xS \dircong Q' \seq xS' = Q_1$ and $P \simplred Q'' \seq xS = Q_2$.
          By induction hypothesis, there exists an $R$ such that $Q' \simplred R$
          and $Q'' \dircong R$. Thus,
          \[
            Q_1 \simplred R \seq xS' \quad \text{and} \quad
            Q_2 \equiv R \seq xS'.
          \]
      \end{enumerate}
    \item (\textsc{dc-cut-comm}) \\
      Again, we proceed by inversion on the derivation of $P \simplred Q_2$.
      We only highlight the subcase \textsc{sr-axcut-l}; the remaining cases
      follow similarly or by straightforward use of the induction hypothesis.

      We are looking at the following situation:
      \[
        P = \parcomp{x}{y}{\mathcal{E}[M]}{Q} \dircong \parcomp{y}{x}{Q'}{P'} = Q_1
      \]
      with $Q \dircong Q'$ and $\mathcal{E}[M] \dircong P'$, and
      \[
        P \simplred \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, Q, x, y) = Q_2.
      \]
      Using Lemma \ref{lemma:reduct-inver-dircong}, there exist $\mathcal{E}'$ and $M'$
      such that $P' = \mathcal{E}'[M']$, $\mathcal{E} \dircong \mathcal{E}'$ and
      $\mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, Q, x, y) \dircong \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}', M', Q', x, y)$.
      Thus,
      \[
        Q_1 = \parcomp{y}{x}{Q'}{\mathcal{E}'[M']} \simplred \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}', M', Q', x, y)
      \]
      and
      \[
        Q_2 = \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}, M, Q, x, y) \equiv \mathsf{reduct}(\mathcal{E}', M', Q', x, y).
      \]
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

By Lemma \ref{lemma:equiv-in-dircong-star}, we obtain the compatibility of
$\parred$ with $\equiv$.

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~Compatibility of parallel reduction with structural congruence] \label{lemma:parred-compatible-struct-cong}
  Let $\Gamma \vdash P$ be a well-typed process.
  If $P \equiv Q_1$ and $P \parred Q_2$, then $Q_1 \parred R$ and $Q_2 \equiv R$ for some $R$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~Diamond property for parallel reduction] \label{lemma:church-rosser-for-parred}
  Let $\Gamma \vdash P$ be a well-typed process.
  If $P \parred Q$ and $P \parred N$, then $Q \parred R$ and $N \parred R$ for some $R$, pictorially
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}[
        row sep={1.5cm,between origins},
        column sep={1.5cm,between origins}
      ]
          & \zb{P} \arrow[dl, "\parred"'] \arrow[dr, "\parred"] & \\
      \zb{Q} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\parred"'] &   & \zb{N} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\parred"] \\
          & \zb{R} &
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Immediate consequence of Lemma \ref{lemma:roncile-simplered} and \ref{lemma:parred-compatible-struct-cong}.
\end{proof}

\section{Church-Rosser}
With the diamond property for single-step parallel reduction established in
Lemma \ref{lemma:church-rosser-for-parred}, we can now generalize this result to its transitive closure.
Following standard techniques from rewriting theory, we proceed in two stages.
First, we establish the \emph{strip lemma} as an intermediate result, which
reconciles a diverging reduction between a single parallel reduction
and a multi-step sequence.

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~Strip lemma] \label{lemma:strip-lemma}
  Let $\Gamma \vdash P$ be a well-typed process.
  If $P \parred Q$ and $P \parred^* N$, then $Q \parred^* R$ and $N \parred R$ for some $R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By induction on the length of the reduction sequence $P \parred^* N$
  and repeated use of Lemma \ref{lemma:church-rosser-for-parred}, pictorially
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[
      row sep={1.5cm,between origins},
      column sep={1.5cm,between origins}
    ]
     & \zb{P} \arrow[dl, "\parred"'] \arrow[dr, "\parred"] & & & \\
    \zb{Q} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\parred"'] & & \zb{N_1} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\parred"] \arrow[dr, "\parred"] & & \\
     & \zb{T_1} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\parred"'] & & \zb{N_2} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\parred"] \arrow[dr, dotted, "\parred"] & \\
     &     & \zb{T_2} \arrow[dr, dotted, "\parred"'] & & \zb{N_n} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\parred"] \\
     &     &     & \zb{R} &
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
\end{proof}

Having shown that a single step of $\parred$ commutes with its transitive closure,
we can now prove the diamond property for $\parred^*$ by inductively applying
the strip lemma to fill out the larger diamond.

\begin{lemma}[\rocqicon~Diamond property for $\parred^*$] \label{lemma:parred-star-diamond}
  Let $\Gamma \vdash P$ be a well-typed process.
  If $P \parred^* Q$ and $P \parred^* N$, then $Q \parred^* R$ and $N \parred^* R$ for some $R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By induction on the length of the reduction sequence $P \parred^* M$
  and repeated use of Lemma \ref{lemma:strip-lemma}. Pictorially:
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[
      row sep={1.5cm,between origins},
      column sep={1.5cm,between origins}
    ]
    &       &       & \zb{P} \arrow[dl, "\parred"'] \arrow[dr, "\parred"]     &       &       &    \\
    &       & \zb{\cdot} \arrow[dl, dotted, "\parred"'] \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow] &       & \zb{\cdot} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow] \arrow[dr, dotted, "\parred"] &       &    \\
    & \zb{\cdot} \arrow[dl, "\parred"']  &       & \zb{\cdot} \arrow[dr, dotted] &       & \zb{\cdot} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow] \arrow[dr, "\parred"] &    \\
  \zb{Q} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\parred"'] &       & \zb{\phantom{\cdot}} &       & \cdot  \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow] &       & \zb{N} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\parred"]  \\
    & \zb{\cdot} \arrow[dr, dotted, "\parred"'] &       & \zb{\phantom{\cdot}} &       & \zb{\cdot} \arrow[dl, dotted, "\parred"] &    \\
    &       & \zb{\cdot} \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow, "\parred"'] &       & \zb{\cdot} \arrow[dl, dashrightarrow, "\parred"] &       &    \\
    &       &       & \zb{R}     &       &       &
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
\end{proof}

Finally, we obtain the main result.
Because we established earlier (Lemma \ref{lemma:parred-red-coincides}) that
normal reduction ($\red$) coincides with the transitive closure of parallel reduction ($\parred^*$),
the confluence of $\parred^*$ immediately implies the Church-Rosser property.

\begin{theorem}[\rocqicon~Church-Rosser] \label{theorem:church-rosser}
  Let $\Gamma \vdash P$ be a well-typed process.
  If $P \red Q$ and $P \red N$, then $Q \red R$ and $N \red R$ for some $R$.
\end{theorem}
